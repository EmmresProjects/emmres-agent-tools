# Retraction and correction practice in a small journal (fixture source A)

## Abstract

This study examines retraction and correction practice in a regional journal between 2015 and 2022. We analysed 412 published articles and found that 9 articles were retracted, representing 2.2% of the sample. Retractions were announced within a median of 148 days after the correction notice.

## Methods

We conducted a retrospective cohort study of all articles published in the journal from 2015 to 2022. Two coders screened each notice for retraction, correction, or expression of concern. The primary outcome measure was the interval between the publication date and the notice date.

## Findings

The journal retracted the paper after an investigation into data fabrication. Corrections are published with a DOI and are linked to the original article. Peer review records are retained for ten years under the policy.

Nine of 412 articles were retracted, and five of those nine retractions cited image duplication. The median interval was 148 days, with a range of 32 to 611 days. The journal published 27 corrections in the same period.

## Discussion

Retraction practice in the journal is consistent with international guidance, although the median interval is longer than the 90 days recommended in the 2019 guidance. A limitation is that we relied on published notices and could not verify the underlying investigations. Our findings may not generalise beyond regional journals with small editorial teams.
